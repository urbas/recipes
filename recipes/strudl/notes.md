# Štrudl Notes

Running log of changes, adaptations, and improvements made each time the
štrudl is baked.

## 2026-07-29

- Water temperature: forgot to use lukewarm water — used whatever came out
  of the cold tap instead. Might matter for how well the dough comes
  together/stretches; keep an eye on this and compare to previous attempts.

- Apples/sugar: using Granny Smith apples this time instead of the Braeburn
  from last time. Braeburn is sweeter, so used 172g of sugar instead of the
  150g used previously, to compensate for the more tart Granny Smith. To be
  seen whether 172g ends up too much or too little.

- Apples: 15 Granny Smith apples from Aldi weighed 2.088 kg in total.

- Cinnamon: used 1.5 heaped teaspoons.

- Oil/butter mixture (for spreading on the dough and over the apples): 53g
  butter + 100g sunflower oil.

- Cloth setup: had the cloth only partially unfolded on the table while
  stretching, which turned out to be a problem. Much better to fully unfold
  the cloth so it hangs evenly on both sides of the table — if the dough
  migrates to one side while stretching, you can pull the cloth to bring it
  back to the centre. Updated the recipe instructions to do this from the
  start next time.

- Baking: took 16 minutes at 200°C for the crust to brown sufficiently
  (recipe estimate was ~15 min, so close). Turned down to just below 170°C
  (recipe says 160°C) after that. About two thirds of the way through the
  remaining bake, lowered further to 160°C. Total time in the oven was 50
  minutes. To be seen whether this stepped-down temperature approach makes
  a noticeable difference.

- Apples: Granny Smith turned out quite juicy — didn't squeeze them as much
  as the Braeburn last time, and some juice was coming out and bubbling
  during baking. To be seen how this affects the result; may need to
  squeeze Granny Smith more firmly than Braeburn despite the general advice
  not to over-squeeze.

- Tasting verdict: turned out pretty well — approved recipe, thumbs up
  from Amy.

- Sugar verdict: ~170g worked well with Granny Smith apples (used 172g).
  Confirms the earlier "to be seen" question — this amount is a good match
  for the more tart Granny Smith.

## 2026-07-25

- Apples: used Braeburn this time (12 Waitrose Braeburn apples). Weighed
  1.818 kg (not quite the usual 2 kg). To be seen how it goes, and whether
  Braeburn is a good apple type for this.

- Flour: used 526g of Waitrose Duchy Organic Strong White Bread Flour (recipe
  says "plain flour" — used bread flour instead, to be seen how it affects
  the dough).

- Vinegar and oil: used Sainsbury's white wine vinegar and sunflower oil.

- Dough problem: with the Duchy Organic Strong White Bread Flour, the dough
  did not come out soft and "gladko"/silky as expected — the ratios seem off.
  Suspect the bread flour (higher gluten/protein than plain flour) is the
  culprit. Retried with Waitrose Regenerative Plain White Flour instead —
  much better result this time, dough came out soft and silky as expected.
  Confirms: use plain flour, not strong/bread flour, for this recipe.

- Mixing: the dough came off the walls of the bowl fairly quickly. However,
  when stopping the mixer to take the dough out, it kept gluing itself to
  the bottom of the bowl. Kept mixing a bit longer and repeated the check a
  few times — eventually it stopped sticking to the bottom, which seemed to
  be the real "done" point (not just coming off the walls). Then kneaded
  briefly by hand on the worktop as well, as mum does.

- Resting: left the dough outside (not in the fridge) to rest for an hour,
  as mum does.

- Cinnamon sugar: mixed 1 heaped teaspoon of ground cinnamon into the 100g
  of sugar ahead of time. Ended up adding more sugar on top anyway during
  assembly. Verdict: 100g wasn't enough — next time use 150g sugar and 2
  teaspoons of cinnamon.

- Stretching the dough: this was the biggest problem/challenge of the whole
  process — still need to learn the technique properly.

- Tasting verdict: tasted alright, but could be better. Feedback for next
  time:
    - Use more sugar: 150g instead of 100g, with 2 teaspoons of cinnamon
    - Don't squeeze the grated apples as much before using them — let the
      filling be more moist
